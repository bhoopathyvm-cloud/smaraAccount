When AI writes a lot of your code, the words in your code matter more than ever.

For over 20 years, domain-driven design has said: use the business's words everywhere, including in the code. That advice was for people. It matters even more for AI. A colleague can ask what a word means. An AI can't. It only knows your project through the words it reads.

A small example from my household money app: my project dictionary said every purchase is recorded as exactly two lines. But a split receipt (Groceries + Household) has three. The app was right; the dictionary was wrong. An AI trusting it could have broken every split purchase.

The mistake was found by an AI that checked each definition against the app, using Matt Pocock's free grill-with-docs skill.

The lesson: write your rules down in plain words, so the AI can follow them and check them.
